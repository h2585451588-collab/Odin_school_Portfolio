Master of Elements

In this world full of elements, people have discovered a way to control them through a set of designed movements called the Elemental Dance. Through different Elemental Dances, people can attack, defend, and perform other actions.

There are five types of elements that people can summon in this game: fire, water, earth, metal, and wood. According to ancient legends, the elements are controlled by the dancer’s emotions.

Straightforward and enthusiastic dancers easily control fire elementals.

Gentle and thoughtful dancers easily control water elementals.

Staunch and introverted dancers easily control earth elementals.

Romantic and unrestrained dancers easily control wood elementals.

Upright and traditional dancers easily control metal elementals.

As a player, you will take on the role of a new student at the School of Elemental Dance, where everything is new and unknown. Will you find friendship and achieve your goal before your time at the school comes to an end?

We will see…

Describe the School

Describe the school’s setting together as a group. Choose wisely, as your characters will be shaped by the environment you create.

Answer the following questions about the school:

Where is the school located?

For example, is it on top of a floating island, secluded in a cave, or near the ocean?

What is the school known for?

For example, is it known for talented but ruthless students, or as a place reserved for only a select few?

Who teaches at the school?

For example, is the teacher a strict but forgiving master or a lighthearted instructor?

Character Sheet Creation

Players need to create a name for their character.

Choose one or two main elements for the character: fire, water, earth, metal, or wood.

Create your character’s backstory. Where do they come from? Are they a student who has traveled far to learn the dance, or simply a curious learner interested in it?

Why does your character want to become an elemental dancer? For example, they may want to use the Elemental Dance to defeat their father’s killer, use an earth dance for gardening, or become a master of the elements and rule the world.

Choose a goal for your character to achieve, such as making a new friend or winning an elemental competition.

What makes your character want to become an elemental dancer? Perhaps they want to become a cook, and a fire dance could help them control fire. Alternatively, a water dance could help them explore the ocean.

What traumatic moment has your character experienced? For example, they may have suffered a childhood accident that burned their face and left a scar, or they may have lost the ability to move their legs after an attack by another elemental dancer.

Elemental Proficiency

If a player begins with only one main element, they start with two levels of proficiency in that element. If a player begins with two main elements, they start with one level of proficiency in each element.

Elemental Dance

Each player may create their own Elemental Dances. If you cannot think of one, you may use the Elemental Dance chart as an example.

Gameplay

There are two phases in the game: the Learning Phase and the Graduation Phase.

Learning Phase

The Learning Phase consists of four big turns. The player who most recently played a sport goes first.

The starting player chooses another player and creates a scenario involving both characters at the school. During each big turn, a special event takes place at the school. Players may choose to interact with one another during the setup of this event.

The active player may ask the other player to teach them one level of proficiency in any element that the other player knows. If the other player refuses, or if the active player decides not to ask, the active player may instead increase their proficiency in an element they already know.

Other players may join the conversation, just as they might in college. Feel free to participate when the characters are discussing something that interests you.

Continue until every player has completed their turn. This marks the end of one big turn.

Example Scenario

The two of you meet on the dance field and introduce yourselves. You discover that you both love a movie starring the same actor. You are moved by the romantic emotions expressed in the film and ask the other player to teach you the wood dance. They agree and show you the dance.

Graduation Phase

During the Graduation Phase, players may choose either to challenge another player to an elemental duel or to perform all the dances they have learned.

After the duel or performance, the player gives a graduation speech about their time at the school. The other players then vote to decide whether the player graduates.

Combat

The two players stand face-to-face with some distance between them. Both players begin the round at the same time and call out the elements they have summoned.

Each element has strengths and weaknesses, which are listed in the elemental table. If one player’s move is strong against the opponent’s move, that player wins the round.

However, if a player summons an element with a proficiency level that is two levels higher than the other player’s, the player with the higher proficiency wins the round, even if their element is normally weak against the opponent’s element.

Both players then describe what happened during the round. A player receives one point for winning a round. The first player to reach three points wins the duel.

Festivals

Turn 1: Elemental Day

A celebration of the discovery of the Elemental Dances. This day is traditionally spent making new friends, baking an elemental cake together, and enjoying the celebration.

Turn 2: Arbor Day

A celebration in which students go outside, plant trees, and protect the natural environment.

Turn 3: Valentine’s Day

A festival for showing love and appreciation to someone you care about.

Turn 4: Spring Festival

A festival for gathering together, playing with fireworks, and performing Elemental Dances for friends and family to welcome a fresh start to the new year.
