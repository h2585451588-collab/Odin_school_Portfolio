Game & Learning Design Case Study · Project 02

Learn

A Classroom Tabletop Roleplaying Game

In Learn, a classroom tabletop roleplaying game, players take on two different roles: one teacher and many students. Together, they learn and build their classroom collaboratively. The game has no specific winning condition. Instead, the goal is to create a classroom that everyone loves and to form a shared story as teacher and students.

I · Setting Up

Setting Up

There are two roles players can choose from:

Teacher

Teacher

To play as the teacher, a player receives a deck of cards featuring different subjects. The teacher’s goal is to describe or introduce a subject to the student players, set a learning goal for the class, and create activities that allow students to explore the subject creatively.

Student

Student

Each student creates a character sheet. Players give their character a unique name, personality, strengths, and behaviors. A student’s role is to follow their chosen character and complete the teacher’s activities in a way that fits who the character is. For example, if a student’s character loves art and the class topic is dogs and cats as human companions, they might create an artwork that expresses their thoughts on the topic.

II · Gameplay

Gameplay

Topic Release

The teacher begins by introducing the topic for the turn. This could involve describing it, showing a picture, or asking students to explore how it appears in their own lives. There is no single correct way to do this; the teacher can be as creative as they like.

Student Research and Study

During this phase, students take time to explore the topic. They might focus on what interests them, build on what they already know, or find related ideas that inspire them. It is okay if what they discover is not directly about the topic, as long as it connects in some way. Students can also ask the teacher questions. If the teacher does not know the answer, that is okay; they can research it together. After exploring, students decide how they want to present what they have learned and what interests them most.

Presenting

Each student may present their work. If a student does not want to present, that is completely fine. The teacher should encourage them gently, but if they still prefer not to share, the group can show support and move on. After each presentation, the group discusses what they appreciated and gives a round of applause to recognize the effort.

Rewarding

After everyone has had the opportunity to present, the group enters the Rewarding phase. Together, they create one or more declarations for the classroom: something symbolic or tangible that reflects the topic they just explored. A declaration can be anything, such as a pair of cozy chairs, a new bookshelf, or a class motto—anything that adds meaning or personality to the shared classroom space.
